# San Diego reproducibility-audit pilot program

## Offer

Provide one complimentary, non-executing reproducibility-readiness audit of a
public research-software repository. The audit inventories documentation,
licensing, tests, environments, provenance, archival/citation metadata, and
release integrity. It does not execute untrusted code and does not judge the
scientific conclusion.

The lab receives:

- a frozen repository and commit reference;
- JSON and readable audit reports;
- prioritized remediation items;
- a short review call if useful; and
- no obligation to endorse, cite, or purchase anything.

In return, ask only for permission to record factual outcomes: delivered,
issues corrected, and—if the recipient chooses—a brief public statement that
the audit was performed. One repository per organization is complimentary.
Further work is separately scoped and paid.

## Priority contacts

### 1. UC San Diego Open Source Program Office

**Why first:** OSPO explicitly promotes open-source best practices,
reproducibility, research-software portfolios, and community participation. It
is the best institutional gateway for selecting a willing UCSD project without
cold-pitching many individual labs.

**Pilot ask:** invite OSPO to nominate one public research repository for a
complimentary audit and critique the rubric.

**Official route:** https://ospo.ucsd.edu/ — `ospo@ucsd.edu`

### 2. UC San Diego Astrophysics & Cosmology

**Why:** direct subject alignment with the flagship DESI cosmology
demonstration. The group works on astrophysics, cosmology, simulations, CMB,
large-scale structure, and dark energy.

**Pilot ask:** audit a public analysis repository chosen by the group; keep
scientific interpretation out of scope unless a researcher independently
chooses to review it.

**Official routes:**

- https://physics.ucsd.edu/research/astrophysics-cosmology
- https://cosmology.ucsd.edu/contact.php

### 3. San Diego Supercomputer Center / Workflows for Data Science

**Why:** the center specializes in scientific workflows, provenance,
distributed computing, and reproducible computational science. This is the
strongest methodological fit and the highest-quality source of adversarial
feedback.

**Pilot ask:** request rubric feedback or a small public workflow repository
whose maintainers want an external static audit.

**Official route:** https://www.sdsc.edu/research/experts/altintas_ilkay.html

### 4. UC San Diego Center for Computational Biology & Bioinformatics

**Why:** CCBB develops open-source computational tools and supports long-term
research collaborations. Its repositories offer a useful test outside
cosmology.

**Pilot ask:** audit one maintained public tool selected by CCBB through its
consultation/contact route.

**Official routes:**

- https://compbio.ucsd.edu/research/software/
- https://compbio.ucsd.edu/about/contact/

### 5. San Diego State Computational Science Research Center

**Why:** CSRC spans astronomy, biology, chemistry, mathematics, physics, and
engineering, with explicit emphasis on robust computational methods,
documentation, and scientific integrity.

**Pilot ask:** audit one public student or center software repository and
provide a reusable readiness report.

**Official route:** https://www.csrc.sdsu.edu/contact/ — `csrc@sdsu.edu`

### 6. UC San Diego Knight Lab

**Why:** the lab publicly commits to open data and open-source tools and would
provide a demanding life-science test of the general audit.

**Pilot ask:** offer a static audit of a small, actively maintained public
repository selected by a maintainer.

**Official route:** https://knightlab.ucsd.edu/

## Outreach order

1. Send the OSPO gateway note.
2. Send one domain-specific cosmology note.
3. Wait five business days and incorporate feedback.
4. Approach SDSC/WoRDS and CCBB with the improved example report.
5. Approach SDSU CSRC and Knight Lab only after at least one pilot has been
   delivered successfully.

Do not mass-email. Every message should identify a specific fit, link the
public methodology, and make refusal effortless.
